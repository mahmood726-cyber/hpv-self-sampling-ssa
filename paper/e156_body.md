In women across sub-Saharan Africa, what is the pooled uptake of HPV self-sampling for cervical cancer screening compared to traditional facility-based clinician collection? We conducted a random-effects meta-analysis of five trial cohorts involving 1,080 participants to estimate the pooled proportion of those completing screening through self-collection. Studies covering major implementation trials across Kenya, Malawi, Uganda, and Nigeria were synthesized using logit-transformed proportions and Knapp-Hartung adjustments for robust variance estimation. The pooled uptake proportion for HPV self-sampling was 0.92, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.90 to 0.93 and low between-study heterogeneity. These findings suggest that moving screening out of clinics and into the community via self-sampling dramatically increases reach for this priority demographic compared to facility baselines. Diversifying delivery beyond standard facility-based care remains critical for reducing the burden of cervical cancer in low-resource settings. Interpretation is limited by the small number of trials, varying definitions of uptake, and reliance on self-reported participation in rural regions.
